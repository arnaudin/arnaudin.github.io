I'd recommend _The Shape of Design_ to any designer, and to anyone curious about design. Don't expect a practical how-to. It's a short, philosophical book about *why* we make things, written as a series of witty, metaphorical meditations. A few sections come off as self-indulgent, but it's a quick read, and it will make you think harder about design, which (if you've found yourself here) is never a bad thing.

A few of my Kindle highlights:

> To be human is to tinker, to envision a better condition, and decide to work toward it by shaping the world around us.

> The Shakers have a proverb that says, “Do not make something unless it is both necessary and useful; but if it is both, do not hesitate to make it beautiful.”

> We should be iterating on how we answer our needs, and not necessarily on the way our old solutions have taken shape.

> All of this implies that design is a field of outcomes and consequences more than one of artifacts.

> Design gains the ability to nourish when it acts as a gift rather than as something to create yearning. We get to close loops of desire rather than open new ones.

> The world shapes us, and we get to shape the world.

Get it free at *[The Shape of Design](http://www.shapeofdesignbook.com/)*.
